# Sources, credits and accuracy

Birdway's application, architectural shaders, ambient music and street props are original. The planar-reflection camera follows the MIT-licensed Three.js Water implementation. The game uses bundled data and assets; it does not contact a map or asset service at runtime.

- **Building footprints and baseline heights:** NYC Open Data, Building Footprints (`5zhs-2jue`), using the snapshot redistributed by [anchitjaincfa/manhattan-3d](https://github.com/anchitjaincfa/manhattan-3d). See the [NYC dataset](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/City-Government/Building-Footprints/5zhs-2jue) and [official metadata](https://github.com/CityOfNewYork/nyc-geo-metadata/blob/main/Metadata/Metadata_BuildingFootprints.md). The playable subset contains 20,868 footprints, quantized to about one metre. Heights supplied in feet were converted to metres; missing heights use a 4 m fallback.
- **Surveyed roof geometry:** 19,666 footprints are matched to the 2014 NYC 3D Building Model LOD2 roof geometry distributed in [rolyatmax/nyc-buildings](https://github.com/rolyatmax/nyc-buildings). The match uses location and dimensions; it keeps the newer footprint and fallback roof height when a reliable survey match is unavailable. The repository's model data is used, not its application code.
- **Building age and class:** Year-built and building-class fields come from the 2017 MapPLUTO dataset published by the NYC Department of City Planning. These fields select among eight procedural façade styles; the façades are generated approximations, not source imagery or surveyed façade models. See [NYC Planning's PLUTO data page](https://www.nyc.gov/site/planning/data-maps/open-data/dwn-pluto-mappluto.page).
- **Parks, plazas, paths and trees:** Derived from the Manhattan OpenStreetMap PBF extracts at [urban-toolkit/autark: lower_mnt.osm.pbf](https://github.com/urban-toolkit/autark/blob/main/gallery/public/data/lower_mnt.osm.pbf) and [urban-toolkit/autark: mnt.osm.pbf](https://github.com/urban-toolkit/autark/blob/main/usecases/public/data/mnt.osm.pbf). The bundled `data/parks.json` contains 7,099 park and plaza polygons, 22,054 mapped paths and 13,504 mapped tree points; the renderer combines a sample of these park/interior tree points with the street-tree census below. © [OpenStreetMap contributors](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). OpenStreetMap data is available under the [Open Database License 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/). `data/parks.json` is a derivative database and is distributed under ODbL 1.0.
- **Street-tree locations and species:** NYC Department of Parks and Recreation, [2015 Street Tree Census](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/Environment/2015-Street-Tree-Census-Tree-Data/uvpi-gqnh), dataset `uvpi-gqnh`, accessed October 10, 2026. The bundled `data/street-trees.json` contains 23,016 records marked Alive, cropped to Manhattan below the mapped 59th Street boundary. Coordinates and species names are retained from this historical census; tree sizes and canopy geometry are inferred and current tree survival is unknown. NYC data is subject to [NYC Open Data Terms of Use](https://opendata.cityofnewyork.us/overview/#termsofuse). `tools/prepare-street-trees.py` reproduces the crop.
- **Rooftop water tanks:** Original batched geometry, placed on 105 suitable prewar apartment roofs using PLUTO building age/class and sampled roof heights. Locations are plausible dressing rather than an inventory of confirmed tanks at each address.
- **Land and streets:** NYC Department of City Planning borough boundaries, from [dwillis/nyc-maps](https://github.com/dwillis/nyc-maps/blob/master/boroughs.geojson), and geographic street centerlines mirrored in [fillerwriter/nyc-streets](https://github.com/fillerwriter/nyc-streets/blob/master/nyc-streets.geojson). Centerline fields follow US Census TIGER/Line. Coastline vertices are simplified to roughly 5 m spacing. This is a historical snapshot, not a current tide line.
- **Pigeon model:** The CC0 Blender source, `assets/pigeon/source.blend`, is [Low poly 3D Pigeon model (rigged + animated)](https://opengameart.org/content/low-poly-3d-pigeon-model-rigged-animated-untextured) by mujtaba-io, downloaded and supplied by the user. `assets/pigeon/pigeon.glb` contains the browser-ready rigged adaptation: a smoothed silhouette, vertex-colored plumage, amber eyes, adapted wing-flap/idle/leg-tuck motion, and added brisk walking, pecking, tucked-wing/tail, extended glide and landing flare poses. Reproduce it with Blender using `blender -b assets/pigeon/source.blend --python tools/prepare-pigeon.py`. The adapted model remains CC0.
- **Three.js:** Vendored v0.180.0, MIT license (see `vendor/THREE-LICENSE.txt`).
- **Photographic surface materials:** CC0 ambientCG brick, concrete, metal, asphalt, turf and soil maps, obtained from the packs mirrored in [fps-buildings-env-kit](https://github.com/petroulacl/fps-buildings-env-kit). Downsized originals, derived facade atlases, IDs and licenses are recorded in [assets/facades/README.txt](assets/facades/README.txt) and [assets/ground/CREDITS.md](assets/ground/CREDITS.md). `tools/prepare-facade-textures.py` rebuilds the facade atlases offline. Textures represent material families, not the actual facade imagery of each address.
- **Grass PBR material:** [Leafy Grass](https://polyhaven.com/a/leafy_grass) by Charlotte Baglioni, Poly Haven, CC0. Locally bundled 1k albedo, OpenGL normal, roughness and height maps. These are a turf material reference, not imagery of a particular NYC park. Nearby curved blades, wind and distance fading are original shaders/geometry. See [assets/ground/CREDITS.md](assets/ground/CREDITS.md).
- **River water:** Original filtered directional waves, tidal-water absorption and Fresnel shader, with the normal texture from the MIT-licensed [Three.js ocean example](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r180/examples/webgl_shaders_ocean.html). The planar reflection camera adapts [Three.js Water](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r180/examples/jsm/objects/Water.js); its MIT license is bundled at `vendor/THREE-LICENSE.txt`. Reflection updates are capped at 12 Hz and 512 px, enabled only in High quality. Coast shading uses a baked distance texture derived from the bundled land boundaries; tidal color lanes are procedural approximations.
- **Harbor boats:** Original lightweight ferry/workboat geometry and foam wakes. Six boats follow water-only routes checked against bundled land boundaries. Silhouettes and routes suggest NYC harbor traffic; they do not represent live vessels or exact ferry schedules.
- **Foliage and bark:** Leaf atlases by Daniel Greenheck from [EZ-Tree](https://github.com/dgreenheck/ez-tree), MIT; oak bark derives from Poly Haven's CC0 Bark Brown 02. Close conifers also use a trimmed CC0 [Pine Tree 01 model](https://polyhaven.com/a/pine_tree_01) by Rob Tuytel and Rico Cilliers. See [assets/trees/CREDITS.md](assets/trees/CREDITS.md) and the bundled EZ-Tree MIT license. Other tree forms use Birdway-generated branching meshes with photographed foliage cards, shaped by NYC's 2015 street-tree census where species are recorded; OSM park trees retain the earlier species approximations.

The playable building and OSM subset covers Manhattan south of an approximate line along 59th Street. Neighboring shorelines provide distant context without full building coverage. Survey matching cannot represent every demolished or rebuilt building. Current construction, roof setbacks, façade materials, roads, park surfaces and landmark structures are simplified or stylized. One World Trade Center, landmark crowns, the Brooklyn Bridge, Washington Square Arch and High Line walkway include hand-built approximations.

To regenerate the data, `tools/prepare-map.py` takes a footprint payload, borough GeoJSON and streets GeoJSON; `tools/prepare-detail.py` matches the 2014 roof model and 2017 PLUTO metadata; `tools/prepare-parks.py` extracts OSM features from a Manhattan PBF. The first script uses only the Python standard library. The detail script requires NumPy and pyproj; the OSM script requires osmium. Source datasets retain their original terms; attribution does not imply endorsement by NYC or OpenStreetMap contributors.
